Nothing to sign up for.

Todo apps have grown into workspaces: accounts, projects, boards, upgrades. Chalk wants one keystroke. It's an app, not a service: your list is a file on your Mac.

  • LocalYour todos never leave your Mac. No account, no servers, nothing collected. The only time Chalk goes online is to check for updates.
  • Plain wordsWrite it like a note. pay rent friday 5pm is a todo with a deadline. Add remind 9am or #home and Chalk reads those too.
  • Out of the wayMenu bar only. No Dock icon, no window to manage, nothing to tidy up.
  • Looks after itselfUpdates itself. New versions install in the background while Chalk is closed, and your list is backed up every day.

Install it in a minute.

Download, drag, open. That's all of it.

  1. Download the .dmg

    Chalk.dmg, one file for Apple Silicon and Intel. Made for macOS 15 (Sequoia), and works on macOS 13 (Ventura) and later. Every version is on GitHub.

  2. Drag Chalk into Applications

    Open the .dmg and drag the icon across. From then on, Chalk updates itself.

  3. Open it

    Chalk sits in the menu bar. Press ⌘⇧K from any app to bring it up.

  4. Allow notifications

    So deadlines and reminders reach you.

Checked by Apple

Chalk is signed with my Apple Developer ID and notarized: before you download a version, Apple has checked it for malware. So it opens like any other app, without a warning.

Updates are signed too, and Chalk only installs ones that come from me.

Where are my todos stored?

On your Mac, in a local database, with a backup made every day. No account, nothing to sign in to, and nothing is sent anywhere. The folder is ~/Library/Application Support/com.cautiouslyfound.chalk (the backups are in Backups inside it).

Does Chalk collect anything?

No. There's no analytics and no account. The only thing Chalk sends is a daily request to GitHub to check for an update, which, like visiting any website, shows GitHub your IP address. This website keeps rough counts with GoatCounter: visits, which buttons get clicked, and roughly how long people stay. No cookies, no IP addresses kept, and nothing that follows you to other sites.

How do I update?

You don't need to. Once a day Chalk checks for a new version and installs it in the background, while it's closed, and your todos and settings are kept. To check now, choose Check for Updates… from its menu bar icon. On a Mac where another account installed Chalk, updating needs an administrator's password, and Chalk tells you why first. Coming from Chalk 3? The first time Chalk 4 opens, it brings your todos, reminders, settings and scratchpad over by itself.

How do I uninstall?

Quit Chalk from its menu bar icon and delete it from Applications. To remove your todos and settings too, delete the folder above (and com.cautiouslyfound.chalkv3 next to it, if you used Chalk 3).

Can it show in the Dock?

By default it lives only in the menu bar. Turn on Show in Dock in Settings (⌘S) and it appears in the Dock and ⌘Tab too.

What can I change in Settings?

Appearance (system, light or dark), font size from 12 to 20px, when deadline notifications arrive (at the deadline, a set time before, both, or none), notification sound, command highlighting, the shortcut hints in the footer, showing in the Dock, launching at login, and the two shortcuts that work from any app.
